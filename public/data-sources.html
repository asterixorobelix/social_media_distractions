Data Sources

Research citations used to compile social media usage statistics

Primary Sources

1. Pew Research Center - Americans' Social Media Use 2025

URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/

Key Data:

  • Platform usage by demographics
  • Gender differences in social media consumption
  • Teen and young adult usage patterns

2. Statista - Time Spent on Social Media by Age (U.S. 2024)

URL: https://www.statista.com/statistics/1484565/time-spent-social-media-us-by-age/

Key Data:

  • Ages 18-24: 186 minutes/day (3 hours 6 minutes)
  • Ages 25-34: 140 minutes/day (2 hours 20 minutes)
  • Platform-specific breakdowns (TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, Facebook)
  • Gender gap data (~15 minutes more for women)

3. Common Sense Media - Social Media & Youth Media Use Research

URL: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research

Key Data:

  • Teen girls: 1 hour 32 minutes/day
  • Teen boys: 52 minutes/day
  • Platform preferences by gender
  • Control and usage concerns

4. Academic Research - Social Media Distraction Studies

URLs:

  • https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15213269.2021.1959350
  • https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8674581/

Key Data:

  • Ages 18-29: 50% report social media distracts them from daily tasks
  • Average notifications: ~23 messages/day for ages 16-25
  • Distraction frequency and impact on productivity

5. Gallup Poll - Teen Social Media Usage

URL: https://news.gallup.com/poll/512576/teens-spend-average-hours-social-media-per-day.aspx

Key Data:

  • Teens: Average 4.8 hours/day on social media
  • Mental health correlations

Data Compilation Notes

  • All statistics represent averages compiled from multiple research sources
  • Individual experiences may vary significantly
  • Data current as of January 2026
  • Gender-specific data based on binary reporting in research studies
  • Age groupings: 13-17, 18-24, 25-34, 35-44

Disclaimer

The statistics displayed on this website are educational estimates based on publicly available research. They represent aggregate trends and may not reflect individual experiences. Data sources are peer-reviewed studies, government-recognized research organizations, and reputable media research institutions.
